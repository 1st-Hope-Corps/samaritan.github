## What We Build

### 🔹 Samaritan Works App (In Development)

Samaritan Works is an actively evolving open-source initiative.

The current repository contains legacy code provided for reference, exploration, and community contribution. An updated and modernized version of the application is under active development and will be published to this repository as it becomes available.

We encourage developers, educators, and mission-aligned contributors to review the existing codebase and participate in shaping the next generation of the Samaritan platform.


**Bringing Valor and Kindness to Scale**

Samaritan Works is a free, open-source mobile application that empowers students and educators to recognize and reward acts of service within their communities.

Through the issuance of digital **Samaritan Citations**, students and teachers highlight individuals performing meaningful works of kindness, leadership, and community service.

Participants who report or perform good works earn **Valiant Tokens**, which can be redeemed for eGift cards and rewards provided by participating local and national merchants.

---

#### How It Works

Samaritan Works webapp is designed for accessibility and ease of use:

- No download required  
- Compatible with all smartphones (iPhone, Samsung, and others)  
- Accessible via standard web browsers (Chrome, Firefox, etc.)  

The platform provides a seamless digital experience where users can:

- Issue and receive Samaritan Citations  
- Track earned Valiant Tokens  
- Redeem awards through an Awards Account  
- View community stories of kindness and impact  
- Engage in a positive, service-driven ecosystem  

---

#### Community Roles

Users can participate in multiple ways:

- **Reporter** – Recognize and document acts of service  
- **Samaritan** – Perform works of kindness and leadership  
- **Reviewer** – Provide thoughtful evaluation and verification  
- **Booster** – Encourage and amplify positive community impact  

Whether contributing minutes or hours, every action strengthens the culture of service.

Samaritan Works leverages technology to celebrate character, encourage civic responsibility, and amplify positive behavior at scale.

### iDetect (Coming Soon)

iDetect is a machine learning–based analysis system designed to evaluate eye imagery captured via smartphone for potential COVID-19 screening research applications.

Originally developed as a patent-pending innovation, the iDetect methodology and associated codebase have now been released as part of our open-source initiative. The project reflects our commitment to responsible AI research, transparency, and collaborative advancement.

The entirety of the iDetect available codebase will be uploaded to this repository as time permits, including model architecture components, preprocessing pipelines, and supporting documentation.

---

#### Concept Overview

iDetect explores a computer-implemented method for performing coronavirus-related screening using a **Deep Convolutional Neural Network (DCNN)**.

The system is designed to:

1. Receive examination data from a user’s mobile computing device, including:
   - Device identification metadata  
   - An initial eye image captured via fundus photography or ocular region imaging using the device’s optical sensor  

2. Pre-process the captured eye image to generate an enhanced and normalized processed image  

3. Assess the processed image by inputting it into a trained machine learning model capable of detecting visual patterns associated with potential coronavirus-related indicators  

4. Return the analytical assessment result, along with device identification information, to the originating device or another designated electronic system  

---

#### Technical Foundation

The iDetect system leverages:

- Deep Convolutional Neural Networks (DCNNs)  
- Computer vision preprocessing pipelines  
- Mobile-device image acquisition workflows  
- Structured metadata handling  
- Secure result transmission architecture  

---

#### Research and Development Status

iDetect is currently in development and intended for research and exploratory purposes.  
It is not a substitute for clinical diagnostic testing.

The project reflects our commitment to responsible AI innovation, applied machine learning, and mobile-accessible health technology research.
